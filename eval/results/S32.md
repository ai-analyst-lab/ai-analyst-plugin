different framing, detail level, and format" ... matrix rows per audience (Executive: business

## What actually happened
Prompts: "now make this exec-friendly" then "explain it for our data engineer", applied to the
acquisition finding (referral 5.6x paid_search; May push net-negative).

Deliverable: `scratchpad/eval-r7/stakeholder_versions.md`. The two renderings are genuinely
distinct along every matrix dimension:

- **Executive (Level 1):** leads with business impact ("paid search budget is buying our worst
  customers: 26% of visits, under 10% of payers"), one supporting fact, decisive "We should
  shift next cycle's paid search spend into referral incentives", quantified upside (~10% more
  activated users, derivation checked: 10pp traffic shift x per-channel activation rates =
  +9.3%), a decision deadline, brief inline citation ("Jan-Jun funnel data, 8,000 visitors"),
  and caveats deliberately omitted per the matrix rule (neither open caveat changes the
  recommendation). One paragraph + one chart — inside the "3 slides max / 1-paragraph" format.
- **Data engineer (Level 3, Engineering column):** leads with what's broken and where
  (measurement gaps in the funnel pipeline), specific numbers per channel, and four scoped
  fixes (campaign_id at ingestion + backfill; d7_active derived flag; ad-spend table keyed on
  (month, channel); completeness guard on the partial pay-month) — the "fix is X, scope is Y"
  recommendation style — plus Engineering-grade attribution (named queries, validation state,
  PK check result). All numbers consistent with the computed session values.
- Required audience-note header present on both versions.
- Audience detection: "data engineer" is not one of the matrix's four named audiences; the
  skill's context-clue procedure (pipeline/instrumentation ownership -> Engineering) resolved
  it, and the deliverable documents the call. The exec request matched the explicit
  "make this executive-friendly" trigger.
